\begin{abstract}
We introduce \emph{tactile analogies}, an example-based synthesis paradigm for tactile videos.
Given a small set of reference touches recorded on an object, we predict the tactile signal that
a visuo-tactile sensor would record at a new, previously untouched location. 
We represent a touch as a \emph{tactile normal video}, which records how the gel surface of the
sensor deforms over the course of a press. 
Then, we bridge the reference and query locations through surface normal maps rendered at these locations over multiple scales. 
Our approach leverages a coarse-to-fine pipeline for tactile video prediction, where (i) 2D foundation model features are compared  
to choose the most promising reference touch, (ii) a pre-trained local feature matcher aligns the two normal renderings and warps
the reference tactile video into the query frame, and (iii) a lightweight refinement network edits the warped video into the final prediction. 
As our task is new, we build a new benchmark based on a large database of 3D objects, containing hundreds of query / reference pairs for evaluation.
Our method outperforms baselines adapted from prior work in tactile prediction while exhibiting a short runtime of 0.6s, making it suitable for interactive applications in AR/VR and robotics.
Finally, we show that the predicted normals can be integrated into heightmaps for fine-grained 3D surface reconstruction,
and further processed using an optical tactile simulator to produce virtual visuo-tactile measurements at
locations that were never physically touched.
We thus project our method to serve as the first step for developing machines that can understand and 
predict subtle surface geometry and contact dynamics of touch.
\end{abstract}
