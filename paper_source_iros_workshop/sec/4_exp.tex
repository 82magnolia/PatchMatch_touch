\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Implementation details}
For retrieval we use a DINOv3 ViT-B/16 encoder~\cite{dinov3} on normal renderings at four times the
sensor footprint, resized to $224\times224$. Coarse alignment uses SuperPoint
keypoints~\cite{superpoint} matched by SuperGlue~\cite{superglue} on the same renderings, with a
homography fitted by RANSAC~\cite{ransac}. The refinement network is based on the smallest ReBotNet
variant~\cite{rebotnet}, trained for 20 epochs with AdamW~\cite{adamw}, batch size 8 and learning
rate $2\times10^{-4}$ with a cosine schedule~\cite{cosine_schedule} on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

\paragraph{Benchmark}
We build a benchmark for tactile analogies from the ObjectFolder dataset~\cite{objectfolder},
simulating tactile normal videos with the Taxim simulator~\cite{taxim} as 50-frame, 5\,fps
press-in-then-withdraw sequences. Every touch comes with view-space renderings of the object
surface at the sensor pose (RGB color, surface normal, curvature and heightmap, at $1\times$,
$2\times$ and $4\times$ the sensor size), giving 17{,}522 touch locations and 876{,}100 tactile
frames over all 1000 objects, with objects 1--950 as the training split and 951--1000 as the
evaluation split. The ground-truth retrieval benchmark pairs each query touch with a given
reference, isolating alignment and refinement from retrieval error, with 8 reference and 8 query
touches per object and 400 query touches in the evaluation split. The full pipeline benchmark
evaluates retrieval as well, collecting on average 15.2 touches on each of 100 objects and holding
out four per object as queries (three when an object has fewer than nine), giving 396 queries
against 1{,}121 references.

\paragraph{Baselines}
Since the task is new, we adapt techniques from prior work in tactile perception. Tactile normal
quilting, adapted from Tactile DreamFusion~\cite{tactiledreamfusion}, tiles the tactile normal of
the reference touch across the object surface by image quilting, renders the view-space normal at
the query pose, integrates it into a height field and renders a tactile video with
Taxim~\cite{taxim}. Implicit neural representations, adapted from
ObjectFolder~\cite{objectfolder}, fit a NeRF-like representation mapping pixel coordinates and
pressing depth to the tactile normal on the reference touches. We report PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS,
averaged over all frames of all evaluation touches.

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports results on both benchmarks, separately for the coarse and the refined
transfer, and Fig.~\ref{fig:gt_retrieval} shows qualitative results at a range of touch locations.
Both stages matter, and both outperform the baselines. On the ground-truth retrieval benchmark the
coarse transfer alone already reaches 22.90\,dB, 4.6\,dB above the stronger baseline and 8.3\,dB
above tactile normal quilting: finding the right geometric correspondence between two normal
renderings and warping the reference video through it already recovers most of what the query touch
looks like, without any learning on the tactile signal itself. The refinement network adds a further
8.3\,dB and halves LPIPS to 0.1085. The two contributions are complementary, as the warp places the
tactile pattern in roughly the right position while the network supplies the gel dynamics and
corrects residual misalignment. The gap is largest on LPIPS, the metric most sensitive to perceptual structure: quilting
synthesizes a plausible texture but cannot make it agree with the surface under the sensor, while
the implicit representation reproduces the touches it was fitted on but must extrapolate to an
unseen location from coordinates alone.

The ordering is unchanged on the full pipeline benchmark, where our refined prediction reaches
31.58\,dB and does not degrade relative to the ground-truth retrieval setting, despite the larger
rotation deviation there and despite references now being chosen automatically. The margin of our
coarse transfer over the implicit representation, however, largely reduces, 21.60\,dB against
21.11\,dB, which we attribute to this benchmark providing multiple references per object, the
regime that representation is designed for. Our refined prediction remains 10.5\,dB ahead, implying
that combining a coarse warp with a network that has learned how the gel behaves leads to accurate
prediction.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

\input{tables/tab_ablation}

Ablating the modality used for retrieval and matching at a fixed $4\times$ field of view, surface
normals score best on all three metrics (27.71\,dB for the coarse transfer, against 27.03\,dB for
curvature, 26.61\,dB for the heightmap and 26.01\,dB for RGB), and unlike RGB they are unaffected by
the object's appearance. Varying the field of view gives no clear winner, as $1\times$ is best on
PSNR by 0.8\,dB while $4\times$ is best on SSIM and LPIPS, so we keep the wider $4\times$ context.

The components of the refinement stage matter far more (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Adding
the refinement network to the coarse transfer gains 3.7\,dB and cuts LPIPS almost in half, to
0.1522. Removing the temporal FiLM conditioning costs 1.5\,dB, confirming that the network
genuinely uses the timestamp: without it a single static normal map has to explain frames at every
stage of the press, so the network can only predict something close to their average. Removing the
normal map concatenation is the most damaging variant, at 25.38\,dB, falling below even the
unrefined coarse transfer on PSNR. Each component is therefore important for accurate transfer.

\subsection{Potential Applications and Runtime}

Since our method predicts tactile surface normals, its output can be turned into surface geometry
with no additional supervision. The predicted normals can be integrated into a heightmap with a
Poisson solver, following GelSight's heightmap extraction~\cite{gelsight}, giving an estimate of
the surface at a location that was never touched, and that heightmap could be passed through the
optical model of Taxim~\cite{taxim} to approximate the color image a visuo-tactile sensor would
have measured. This could aid manipulation
policies trained on visuo-tactile readings, letting an agent rehearse a grasp at candidate contact
points before touching the object.

Measured on an RTX 3090 GPU as the mean of ten timed repeats after a warm-up, with $N=5$ references
and one query, a prediction takes 0.08\,ms for retrieval, 0.021\,s for coarse alignment and
8.5\,ms per frame for refinement (0.42\,s for a 50-frame video), with feature extraction adding
0.06\,s for DINOv3 and 0.08\,s for matching. End to end this is roughly 0.6\,s, suited to
runtime-critical applications such as AR/VR and robotics.
