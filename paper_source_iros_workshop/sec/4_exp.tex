\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Implementation details}
For retrieval we use a DINOv3 ViT-B/16 encoder~\cite{dinov3} on normal renderings at four times
the sensor footprint, resized to $224\times224$, and rank references by cosine similarity between
$\ell_2$-normalized class-token features. Coarse alignment uses SuperPoint
keypoints~\cite{superpoint} matched by SuperGlue~\cite{superglue} on the same $4\times$ normal
renderings, with a homography fitted by RANSAC~\cite{ransac}. The refinement network is based on the smallest ReBotNet variant~\cite{rebotnet}. We train for 20 epochs with AdamW~\cite{adamw}, batch size
8, and learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$ with a cosine schedule~\cite{cosine_schedule}. Training runs on a single RTX 3090 GPU and takes
approximately 32 hours.

\paragraph{Benchmark}
We create a benchmark for evaluating tactile analogies using the ObjectFolder
dataset~\cite{objectfolder}. For every object we generate a set of tactile normal videos recording
the gel normal changes over time, simulated with the Taxim tactile simulator~\cite{taxim}. 
Each touch is a 50-frame video at 5\,fps following a press-in-then-withdraw format. 
Every touch is accompanied by view-space renderings of the object surface at the sensor pose, namely RGB color, surface normal, curvature, heightmap at $1\times$, $2\times$ and $4\times$ the sensor size. In total the benchmark comprises 17{,}522 touch locations and 876{,}100 tactile frames across all 1000
ObjectFolder objects. Objects 1--950 form the training split and objects 951--1000 the evaluation
split.

We define two benchmark settings.

\emph{Ground-truth retrieval benchmark.} Each item is a pair of touches, one designated query and
one designated reference, with their tactile videos and normal renderings. Since the pairing
is given, this setting isolates the coarse alignment and refinement stages from retrieval error.
We simulate 8 reference and 8 query touches per object, giving 16{,}000 touch locations.
The evaluation split contains 400 query touches.

\emph{Full pipeline benchmark.} Here we evaluate the complete pipeline including retrieval. We
collect $K$ touches per object, on average 15.2 touches on each of 100 objects, and 1{,}522
locations in total. We then hold out four touches per
object as queries, three when an object has fewer than nine touches, drawn with a fixed random
seed. This gives 396 query touches evaluated against 1{,}121 reference touches.

\paragraph{Baselines}
Since the task is new, we adapt techniques from prior work in tactile perception.

\emph{Tactile normal quilting}, adapted from Tactile DreamFusion~\cite{tactiledreamfusion}, uses
image quilting to tile the tactile normal of the reference touch across the entire object mesh
surface, renders the view-space normal at the query sensor pose, integrates the normals into a
height field, and renders a tactile video with Taxim~\cite{taxim}.

\emph{Implicit neural representations}, adapted from ObjectFolder~\cite{objectfolder}, train a
NeRF-like implicit representation that takes pixel coordinates and pressing depth as input and
outputs the tactile normal. The representation is fitted per sample on the reference touches and
evaluated at the query locations.

We report PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS, averaged over all frames of all evaluation touches.

\subsection{Performance Comparison}

\subsubsection{Ground-truth retrieval}

\input{tables/tab_gt_retrieval}

Table~\ref{tab:gt_retrieval} reports quantitative results on the ground-truth retrieval benchmark,
separately for the coarse transfer produced by feature-matching alignment alone and for the final
refined transfer. Fig.~\ref{fig:gt_retrieval} shows qualitative results at a range of touch
locations: for each row we show the reference touch, the reference and query normal renderings,
the coarse transfer, our refined prediction, and the ground-truth query touch.

Both stages of our method matter, and both outperform the baselines. The coarse transfer alone already
reaches 22.90\,dB, which is 4.6\,dB above the stronger baseline, the implicit neural
representation, and 8.3\,dB above tactile normal quilting. This implies that simply finding the right
geometric correspondence between two normal renderings and warping the reference video through it
recovers most of what the query touch looks like, without any learning on the tactile signal
itself. The refinement network then adds a further 8.3\,dB, halving LPIPS from 0.2102 to 0.1085.
The two contributions are complementary rather than redundant: the warp places the tactile pattern
in the rouhgly right position, and the network supplies the gel dynamics and corrects the residual
misalignment that the warp leaves behind.

The gap to the baselines is largest on LPIPS, the metric most sensitive to perceptual structure.
Tactile quilting synthesizes a plausible surface texture but has no mechanism to make it agree with the
particular surface under the sensor, so it scores 0.4833. The implicit representation is fitted
per sample and therefore reproduces the reference touches it was trained on, but it must
extrapolate to an unseen location from pixel coordinates and the query location coordinates alone, which could be difficult for previously unseen touch locations.

\subsubsection{Full pipeline}

\input{tables/tab_full_pipeline}

Table~\ref{tab:full_pipeline} reports results on the full pipeline benchmark, which includes
retrieval error, again split into coarse and refined transfer.
Fig.~\ref{fig:full_pipeline} compares our predictions against the baselines frame by frame.

Similar to the ground-truth retrieval case, our refined prediction again outperforms the tested baselines. To elaborate, our refined result does not degrade relative to the ground-truth retrieval setting, despite the larger rotation deviation that exists in the benchmark and reference touches now being chosen automatically via DINOv3.
Further, the margin of our coarse transfer over the implicit neural representation largely reduces,
21.60\,dB against 21.11\,dB, even though the same comparison was 4.6\,dB in the ground-truth
retrieval setting. 
We hypothesize that this is due to the fact that the benchmark provides multiple reference touches per object, which is the regime the per-object implicit representation is designed for. Our refined prediction nonetheless performs consistently, implying that the combination of a coarse warp with a network that has learned how the gel behaves leads to accurate tactile prediction.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

\input{tables/tab_ablation}

We ablate the key components of our method to show their contribution to accurate tactile
transfer. All ablations are run on a 20-object subset of the full pipeline benchmark, and
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS. We study three groups of choices.

First, the \emph{modality} used for retrieval and for feature matching during initial alignment,
with the field of view fixed at $4\times$ the sensor size: surface normals (our default),
RGB color, curvature, and height map. Second, the \emph{field of view} used for retrieval and
matching: $1\times$, $2\times$ and $4\times$. Third, the components of the refinement stage: no
neural refinement at all (i.e.\ the coarse transfer), no temporal FiLM conditioning, and no normal
map concatenation. Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} compares predictions from the last three variants
visually.

\input{figures/fig_ablation}

\paragraph{Modality}
Surface normals are the best of the four modalities on all three metrics, but the spread is
narrow, 1.7\,dB between the best and the worst, and only 0.010 in LPIPS. Normals are nonetheless a sensible default as it outperforms other modalities, and unlike RGB they are unaffected by the object's
appearance.

\paragraph{Field of view}
The three fields of view disagree about which is best depending on the metric, and thus there is not a clear winner. Matching at $1\times$ gives the highest PSNR, 0.8\,dB above $4\times$,
while $4\times$ gives the best SSIM and LPIPS.
A narrow field of view sees only the contact patch, whereas a wide field of view sees the surrounding surface, which makes the match more likely to be found and better conditioned.
We keep $4\times$ as the default because the perceptual metric and SSIM favor it.

\paragraph{Refinement}
We find that the benefits of refinement is quite large compared to the aforementioned attributes for the ablation study. Adding the refinement network to the coarse transfer gains
3.7\,dB and cuts LPIPS almost in half, from 0.2797 to 0.1522. Removing the temporal FiLM
conditioning costs 1.5\,dB and raises LPIPS to 0.1735, confirming that the network genuinely uses
the timestamp: without it, a single static normal map has to explain frames at every stage of the
press, and the network can only predict something close to their average. Removing the normal map
concatenation is by far the most damaging variant, at 25.38\,dB, which falls below even the
unrefined coarse transfer on PSNR while remaining better on LPIPS.
Therefore each component of the refinement network is crucial for accurate tactile transfer.

\subsection{Application in 3D Surface Reconstruction and Visuo-tactile Sensor Simulation}

\input{figures/fig_recon}

Our method predicts tactile surface normals, so its
output can be turned into surface geometry with no additional supervision. We integrate the predicted normals and convert them into a heightmap with a Poisson solver following the heightmap extraction procedure of Gelsight~\cite{gelsight}. The
result is a 3D reconstruction of the object surface at a location that was never physically
touched. Such reconstructions let an embodied agent reason about fine-grained shape properties of
previously untouched regions, and can aid haptic rendering.

Going one step further, feeding the same heightmap through an optical tactile simulator, Taxim's optical
model~\cite{taxim} produces the color image a visuo-tactile sensor would have measured,
namely a virtual visuo-tactile measurement. This could aid manipulation policies that are trained
directly on visuo-tactile sensor readings: an agent could, for instance, rehearse a grasp by
predicting the tactile videos at candidate contact points before touching the object at all.
Fig.~\ref{fig:recon} shows the full chain, from predicted tactile normal
video to the reconstructed 3D surface height map and the simulated color frames. Both steps after the network are post-processing of the predicted normals, so the whole chain runs at deployment
time from a single reference touch.

\subsection{Runtime Analysis}

We report the runtime of our full pipeline, measured on an RTX 3090 GPU as the mean of ten timed
repeats after a warm-up pass. Given $N=5$ reference touches and one query, producing a touch
prediction takes 0.08\,ms for the retrieval phase after DINOv3 feature extraction, 0.021\,s for
coarse alignment after local feature matching, and 8.5\,ms per frame for neural refinement, which
is 0.42\,s for a 50-frame touch video. In addition, for feature extraction it takes 0.06\,s for DINOv3 and 0.08\,s for local feature matching, both of which are single
forward passes of pre-trained networks. End to end, a complete prediction therefore takes
roughly 0.6\,s.
Thus our method is lightweight and well suited to runtime-critical applications such as AR/VR
and robotics.
