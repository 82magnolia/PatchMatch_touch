\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper we introduced \emph{tactile analogies}, the task of synthesising tactile videos at
untouched locations from a set of reference touches, and proposed an example-driven pipeline that
solves it. Given a handful of reference touches on an object, our method retrieves the most
relevant one using self-supervised features of surface normal renderings, aligns it to the query
sensor pose with a pre-trained local feature matcher, and refines the warped video with a
lightweight network conditioned on the query geometry and on where in the press it is. Experiments
on a benchmark we build from ObjectFolder show that our method outperforms all tested baselines in
video prediction accuracy, while remaining fast enough for interactive use. We also demonstrate
that the predicted tactile normals can be used for 3D surface reconstruction and for simulating
visuo-tactile measurements without direct physical contact.
We project our work to serve as an important first step towards building models
that can accurately perceive and understand the complex dynamics of touch.
